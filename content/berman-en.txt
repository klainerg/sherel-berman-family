## The Furman family of Kamianets-Podilskyi

The roots of the Berman family are connected to the Furman family — Фурман — who lived in the Kamianets-Podilskyi area.

The head of the Furman family was Avrum (Abraham) Furman. His children included Eva, Roza and Shmeyra.

Eva Furman — Ева Абрамовна Фурман — married Israel Grigoryevich Berman (Израиль Григорьевич Берман), known in the family as Izia.

Izia was born in 1895.

The couple had three children: Dasha (Даша), born on 10 June 1926; Grisha Berman (Гриша), born in 1928; and Galochka (Галочка), the youngest daughter.

Grisha Berman was Dasha's brother and should not be confused with Grisha Sherel, who would later become her husband.

## The years before the war

Because of Izia's work, the family moved between different cities in the Soviet Union.

According to family memories, they lived, among other places, in Bobruisk (Бобруйск), and later moved to Lviv (Львов).

In Lviv, Izia worked in the administration of a factory or industrial organisation in the fuel sector, near Stryiskyi Park (Стрыйский парк). According to the family's account, they lived opposite his workplace.

The move to Lviv took place at a time when political changes and new borders affected the lives of millions of people in Eastern Europe.

In 1941, Dasha was 15 years old, her brother Grisha was 13, and Galochka was still a little girl.

## June 1941 — the family separates

When Germany invaded the Soviet Union, Lviv became one of the centres of fighting and flight.

According to family memories, Izia was called to a conference in Kyiv at that time. He was later drafted into the Red Army in a military-political role — политрук.

A Soviet military document found in the OBD Memorial database records Israel Grigoryevich Berman, born in 1895, as having been drafted in 1941 and listed as missing since June of that year.[[מקור: memorial]]

At the same time, Eva was forced to flee the city with her three children.

So began a long and difficult journey that lasted for months and changed their lives forever.

## Fleeing Lviv

Family memories describe a journey east from Lviv in a freight train, trying to get away from the advancing German front.

The family reached the Berdychiv (Бердичев) area and then Lysa Hora (Лысая гора). Along the way there were transit stops, encounters with acquaintances and attempts to find a way to continue east.

The account also mentions meeting Izia during one stage of their flight. This is one of the last memories of him before he disappeared.

Eva continued the journey with her children. They travelled by train, on horse-drawn carts and between evacuation locations.

After lengthy wandering, they reached Stalingrad, a city that would become one of the most intense centres of fighting in the Second World War.

## The tragedy on the barge

At a later stage of the evacuation, as the family left the Stalingrad area and headed east, the greatest tragedy of their journey occurred.

Galochka, Eva and Izia's youngest daughter, died on a barge during the journey.

According to the family's account, her body was thrown into the water along the way. No known grave remained to which they could return.

Eva continued with her two surviving children — Dasha and Grisha.

Galochka's death added to the uncertainty about Izia's fate, as he remained missing.

## Life far from home — Yamanka

The family eventually reached the Guryev (Гурьев) area, in present-day Kazakhstan, and settled in the village of Yamanka (Яманка).

According to family memories, a local Russian woman took five evacuated families into her home.

Living conditions were difficult. The evacuees had to adapt to a remote place, shortages and the uncertainty of the war years.

Despite everything, Eva managed to protect her two children and carry on their lives far from the home they had fled.

## Reuniting with Roza

Roza, Eva's sister, also managed to survive the war years.

She was married to Senya Dik (Сеня Дик), and they had three children — Bella, Yasha and Bronya.

In 1943, Roza worked as a nurse in a hospital in Aktyubinsk (Актюбинск), Kazakhstan.

According to the family story, she managed to locate Eva and her children through evacuee records.

When they met, Roza initially struggled to recognise them after the hard years they had endured.

The reunion marked the end of a long period of separation and lack of news.

## Those who did not survive

Not all members of the Furman family managed to escape.

Shmeyra, Eva and Roza's brother, was married to Khona (Хона). They also had children, including Dasha and Grisha — names that recurred in the family.

According to the family's account, Shmeyra and his family were murdered during the German occupation.

After the war, news of their relatives' fate reached the surviving family members. Among those who passed on information was Fania Krayevskaya (Фаня Краевская), who told them that Eva had survived and that members of Shmeyra's family had died.

Gradually, it became clear which relatives had survived and which would never return.

## Returning to life

In 1944, according to family memories, the hospital where some family members were staying was transferred to Crimea.

As the Red Army advanced and areas of Ukraine were liberated, the family members were able to return to the Kamianets-Podilskyi area.

After the war, Eva, Dasha and Grisha continued rebuilding their lives.

There was no such return for Israel Berman, Izia.

In 1946, an official search form was completed for him. The document named his wife, Eva Abramovna Berman, and her address in Gaspra (Гаспра), near Yalta in Crimea.[[מקור: memorial]]

This is one of the most important documents about Izia that remain with us. Even years after his disappearance, the family continued searching for information about his fate.
