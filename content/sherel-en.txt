## The family's roots

The Sherel family — Шерель — was a Jewish family originating from the area around Kamianets-Podilskyi (Каменец-Подольский), a city with a long-established Jewish community in southwestern Ukraine.

Naum’s son, Iosif Naumovich Sherel (Иосиф Наумович Шерель), was born in 1895. He and his wife Olya (Оля) started a family.

## [iosif-early-life] Iosif and Olya

According to family memories, Iosif served as the chairman of a kolkhoz — председатель колхоза — a collective agricultural farm during the Soviet period. It was a managerial position with responsibility for the farm's agricultural activities and workers.

The family also preserved a photograph of Iosif in uniform with several colleagues.

[[תמונה: iosif-sherel-uniform.png | Iosif Sherel in uniform with colleagues — fourth from the left]]

Iosif and Olya had three children: Grigory, Fima and Klava.

Grigory Iosifovich Sherel (Шерель Григорий Иосифович) was born in 1924 in Orinin (Оринин), a town about 16 kilometres northwest of Kamianets-Podilskyi.

Within the family, he was called Grisha.

## [iosif-family-story] Iosif Sherel: Courage, strength and determination

[[תמונה: iosif-sherel-portrait.jpeg | Iosif Naumovich Sherel]]

Iosif Naumovich Sherel (Иосиф Наумович Шерель) was born in 1895. He was tall, broad-built and exceptionally strong. Stories passed down through the family portray him as a courageous, resourceful and determined man who kept his nerve even in great danger.

In the 1920s, during the Civil War, Symon Petliura’s forces took control of the town of Orinin (Оринин). Two Ukrainian fighters known as Haidamaks (Гайдамаки) arrested Iosif and led him into the woods to execute him. Both men were short and carried rifles and sabres. On the way, Iosif snatched one man’s sabre and raised it against them. Startled, both men fled into the woods, forgetting that they carried rifles. Iosif’s courage, strength and quick thinking saved his life. He used to tell the story to his family himself.

In the 1930s, Iosif played a prominent role in the civic and agricultural life of Orinin’s Jewish community. He chaired the Jewish collective farm called Novy Put (Новый Путь), meaning ‘New Way’, known in Yiddish as ‘אַ נײַער וועג’. In that role, he promoted Jewish collective farming in the Soviet Union. He travelled across Ukraine with Pavel Petrovich Postyshev (Павел Петрович Постышев), one of the leading Communist officials in Ukraine at the time. They encouraged Jews to join collective farms and work the land together. Postyshev later fell victim to Stalin’s purges and was executed in 1939, along with other Communist Party leaders.

Iosif retained his remarkable strength into old age. At seventy, he found it hard to settle into retirement and spend long hours at home with nothing to do. He decided to return to work and took a part-time job at a nearby vegetable shop. Even at that age, he would hoist barrels of sauerkraut weighing up to about a hundred kilograms onto his shoulders and carry them himself.

These three stories stayed in the family’s memory. They portray Iosif Sherel as a man of physical strength and resolve, courageous and resourceful, active in Orinin’s Jewish community, and still vigorous in his later years.

## Jewish life in Orinin

Orinin was a town with a substantial Jewish community. The 1939 census counted 1,508 Jews, who made up about a quarter of its population.

The town's Jews worked in trade, crafts and agriculture. A Jewish kolkhoz also operated in the area, during a period when the Soviet authorities encouraged the establishment of collective farms.

Grisha grew up at a time of far-reaching changes in Soviet society. Communist rule influenced daily life, the economy and culture, but the Jewish community continued to maintain family ties and some of its customs.

## The war and the destruction of Orinin

On 22 June 1941, Nazi Germany invaded the Soviet Union. Within just a few weeks, the occupying forces also reached the Kamianets-Podilskyi and Orinin area.

Some Jews managed to evacuate east before the occupation. Many others remained in the town.

Persecution, restrictions and arrests began in the summer of 1941. Orinin's Jews were concentrated in their residential area, which became an open ghetto, and were subjected to forced labour and restrictions on movement.

On 21 June 1942, the Orinin ghetto was liquidated. The Jews were assembled and taken outside the town, where they were murdered in mass shootings. According to later Soviet documentation, about 1,700 Jews were murdered, including refugees and Jews from the surrounding area, although historical estimates vary.

Grisha Sherel survived the war years.

## [sherel-service] Two generations at war, 1941–1945

In 1941, the year Orinin came under occupation, Iosif began serving in the Red Army. He was about 46. His military records list him as Jewish. He was called up through the Liebknecht district military commissariat (Либкнехтовский РВК) in Stavropol Krai.

In the summer of 1942, German forces advanced towards the Don, Rostov and the Caucasus. Iosif was wounded near Rostov-on-Don in July. He received the medal ‘For the Defence of Stalingrad’ («За оборону Сталинграда») for taking part in the defence of the city. The medal appears in the military record for the defence of Stalingrad and is listed as a previous award in his recommendation sheet of July 1945.[[מקור: iosif-stalingrad]]

[[תמונה: iosif-stalingrad-medal.jpeg | Iosif Sherel’s Medal for the Defence of Stalingrad]]

In August 1942, his son Grisha also began serving in the Red Army, aged about 18. In November and December he took part in the defence of the area around Ordzhonikidze (now Vladikavkaz) and Ardon in North Ossetia, where Soviet troops halted the German advance deeper into the Caucasus. On 8 January 1943, Grisha was listed for military transfer to Tbilisi as German forces began withdrawing from the Caucasus.[[מקור: grisha-service]]

By the summer of 1944, Grisha held the rank of yefreytor (Ефрейтор) in the 18th Army’s 30th Separate Trophy Company. In August, the company operated around Dolyna in western Ukraine, collecting and removing equipment and weapons left on the battlefields. His award recommendation describes the recovery of 12 artillery pieces, seven anti-tank rifles and four machine guns. Grisha also suffered two blast concussions during his service.

On 9 August 1944, his commander recommended him for the Order of the Red Star. After review, Grisha received the Medal for Courage («За отвагу»), approved by an order of the 18th Army on 10 September 1944.[[מקור: grisha-service]]

Iosif continued to serve after his injury. In 1945, he was a private in the 92nd Military Construction Detachment. Despite the lasting effects of his wound, his commanders noted the care and quality of his construction work and his completion of assignments ahead of schedule.

On 5 May 1945, Grisha was named in award document No. 425 for the Medal for the Defence of the Caucasus («За оборону Кавказа»), recognising his part in the 1942–1943 campaign.[[מקור: grisha-service]]

[[תמונה: grisha-caucasus-medal.png | Grisha Sherel’s Medal for the Defence of the Caucasus]]

After the war in Europe ended, Iosif’s commanders recommended him for the Medal for Combat Merit («За боевые заслуги»). The recommendation sheet bears dates of 16–17 July 1945.[[מקור: iosif-merit]]

Their service took them through different theatres. Iosif was wounded near Rostov, took part in the defence of Stalingrad and continued in military construction. Grisha helped defend the Caucasus before moving west with the 18th Army into western Ukraine.

[[תמונה: grisha-sherel-uniform.jpeg | Grigory (Grisha) Sherel in uniform with his medals for courage and the defence of the Caucasus]]

Grigory Sherel, Fima Sherel and Klava Giter returned to Orinin after the war. The return is described in a historical source on Orinin’s Jewish community and in family recollections.[[מקור: orinin]]

This fits the family's account of members of the Sherel family who survived the war and returned to the area where they had lived.

## Returning to Orinin

After the area was liberated in 1944, survivors of the Jewish community began returning home. They found a town entirely different from the one they had known before the war.

Grigory Sherel worked as an accountant.

His relatives also continued their lives.

Klava Sherel married Hozkel Giter — Хезкель Гитер (Hozkel Giter).

Klava and Hozkel had a son named Sasha (Саша), who now lives in the United States.

Grisha's brother, Fima Sherel, also started a family. Fima had two children, who live in the United States.

In this way, the branches of the Sherel family continued to grow, and over the years members of later generations also reached the other side of the ocean.
